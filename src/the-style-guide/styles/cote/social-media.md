# Social media voice

Platform-by-platform guidance for posts in Coté's voice. Three different
registers depending on context:

1. **Personal posts** (his own LinkedIn / Mastodon / Bluesky / Twitter feeds, blog promo, video promo). Warm, first-person, "my pals" framing for collaborators.
2. **Company-page posts** (employer-account LinkedIn). Third person, full names, no warm-personal moves. Used when posting from a corporate account about content he co-produced.
3. **Solo standalone-video promo** (LinkedIn promo for a 5-minute talking-head video). Breezier than the analytical default - looser, exclamation marks allowed, soft winking sell.

## Universal rules across platforms

- **Never wrap URLs in `**bold**`.** Auto-linkers grab the asterisks and break the link. Bare URLs.
- **No em-dashes.** Hyphens with spaces.
- **Straight quotes only.**
- **No hashtag spam on personal posts.** Hashtags allowed only on company-page posts where the convention calls for them.

## Personal LinkedIn / Mastodon / Bluesky (default warm voice)

For sharing a video, podcast, blog post, or industry link from his own
feed.

### Mastodon (one sentence + URL)

> My pals @cohost-a and Co-host B go over how AI has improved how you can handle security issues, for app developers: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EXAMPLE

Pattern: one warm sentence naming the guests/topic + bare URL. No hashtags. No quoted SRT lines. Positive framing ("AI has improved...") beats doomer framing ("the math just broke").

### Bluesky (same shape, Bluesky handles)

> My pals @cohost-a.example.com and @cohost-b.bsky.social go over how AI has improved how you can handle security issues, for app developers: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EXAMPLE

Same structure as Mastodon. Use platform-native handles.

### Personal LinkedIn (slightly longer)

A few short paragraphs (2-3 sentences each). No markdown headers. Still
conversational, not promotional. End with a question or provocative
observation, not a call to action.

Voice markers:

- "My pals @[handle] and [name]..."
- "I've been circling a theme this week..."
- Self-deprecating asides.
- One emoji at most, and only if it's earning its keep (👉 pointing at a link is fine; sparkles ✨ are not).

## Solo standalone-video promo on LinkedIn (breezier voice)

When posting his own 5-minute talking-head video. Looser than the
default warm voice.

Markers:

- Inline numbered list with `(1) ... (2) ... (3) ...` rather than separate labeled paragraphs.
- Exclamation marks are fair game: "holy cow!", "I bet you know what I think you should try!"
- Colloquial framings: "gussied up search", "we finally have to pay for this stuff".
- Soft, winking CTA at the end instead of a thought-closer: "I bet you know what I think you should try! 👉 [link]"
- One emoji (the pointing finger) is fine when it's pointing at the link.

When in doubt, draft once analytical and once breezy and pick the one
that sounds more like a guy elbowing the reader.

## Company-page LinkedIn (third-person, no warm framing)

For posts that go out from a corporate account (the company you work
for, not Coté personally). The voice changes entirely.

Rules:

- **Third person throughout.** No "I", no "my pals", no "I think".
- **Full names, no @handles in the body.** "Michael Coté, Co-host A, and Co-host B discuss..." not "@thecote and @cohost-a..."
- **Open with a speaker-attributed quote** if there is one: `"Code is cheap, software is not," Co-host A on last week's [show name].`
- **Body is a plain recap of the angle**, not a personal reaction. No "anyone who's spent any real time with X" or other personal-voice moves.
- **Capitalize words for emphasis** where natural: "everything BUT coding when it comes to AI generated software".
- **Hashtag set is broader and longer** than personal posts. 8-10 hashtags is fine. Casing can be inconsistent across the set - don't force CamelCase across the board.

Footer pattern (after the body):

1. `Full conversation on [Show Name]: <URL>`
2. (Blank line) optional product CTA paragraph if relevant.
3. (Blank line) `We stream live every [day] at [time]: <playlist URL>`
4. (Blank line) hashtag block.

## Twitter / X

When used: short, one-sentence-plus-URL, same as Mastodon. No threads
unless explicitly requested. No hashtags by default.

## Video descriptions (YouTube, Vimeo, etc.)

For Coté's own videos:

- First paragraph: one or two warm sentences framing what the video is about. First person.
- Then: a chapter list / agenda if the video has segments, formatted as `MM:SS Title`.
- Then: relevant links (cited articles, related episodes, his blog), bare URLs.
- Then: guest credits with their handles for at least one platform.

Don't write a marketing blurb. Write what the video is, who's in it,
and where to find the related stuff.

## Blog promo (when announcing a new video / podcast episode on his blog)

For blog posts that exist as a landing page for a new episode:

- Open: "This is an excerpt from our [Show Name last week](URL)." or similar.
- One or two sentences framing what the episode was about, lightly positive.
- Blockquote the top of the YouTube description (or canonical promotional copy) - don't rewrite, reuse.
- Close: "Check out [the full episode](URL), it was fun and packed with good discussion." Or similar warm "go watch it" line.

## What to avoid on all social

- Pundit-voice openers: "Hot take:", "Unpopular opinion:", "This will be controversial but..."
- Cliffhanger openers: "Most people don't know this, but..."
- "🧵 1/" thread starters.
- LinkedIn-storytime: "I was on a flight last Tuesday when a stranger asked me..."
- Anything that reads like a sales-coach LinkedIn post.
- Doomer framing of mundane news.
- Doom-positive both-sides framing of clearly-negative news. Pick one.

## Useful Cote-isms for social

- "My read of this kind of thing is..."
- "(probably)"
- "(checks notes)"
- "Still."
- "Anyway."
- "Tubes of cash."
- "Enterprise sludge."
- "I bet you know what I think you should try!"
