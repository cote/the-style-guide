# Casual voice

The default register for Coté's writing. Use for blog posts, newsletter
prose, personal LinkedIn / Mastodon / Bluesky posts, podcast show notes,
talk titles, and most email replies.

`general.md` covers the voice in broad strokes. This file is the deeper
reference - sentence-level moves, anti-patterns, and worked examples.

## The voice

Coté writes like a tech industry veteran who reads too much philosophy
and watches too much television. Conversational and knowing, occasionally
profane, never performative. The reader is a peer who's been around long
enough to smell bullshit but hasn't become so cynical they've stopped
building things. Humor is dry and observational, not jokey - more raised
eyebrow than punchline.

## Sentence-level moves

### Mix lengths aggressively

Short declarative sentences for emphasis. Longer ones when explaining
systems or connecting ideas. The rhythm matters.

> My read of this kind of thing is that enterprises are struggling to find uses for AI in day-to-day work. Programming is the exception.

> Do work, go home.

> I still think Amazon should buy Anthropic.

### Parenthetical asides for meta-commentary

Signature little editorial interjections that break the fourth wall:

- "(checks notes)"
- "(so I am told)"
- "(probably)"
- "...there's even a Mekko chart!"

Use sparingly enough that each one lands.

### `//` as an editorial pivot

When commenting on a quote, link, or paraphrase, Coté often uses `//`
to shift from description to opinion:

> "Put another way, agents are so good at attacking accidental complexity that they _generate new accidental complexity_..." // There's something in this: if you use your productivity gains to do more instead of going home early, you end up with the same amount of problems as always.

### "My read of this kind of thing is..."

Do not overdo this, but sometimes begin with an observation rather than a thesis statement:

- "My read of this kind of thing is..."
- "I've been circling a theme this week..."
- "I've been thinking about X..."

These are all observation-first openers.

### Start paragraphs with "Still" or "But"

Used as their own paragraph or as a sentence opener. Conversational
hinge between ideas.

> Still. The math is the math.

### Visceral, not academic

"Feel in our bones" beats "evidence suggests". "Tubes of cash" beats
"capital flows". Use the more bodily / concrete option when both work.

### Moderate absolute claims

"Getting very little" rather than "getting nothing". "Almost no one"
rather than "no one". The hedge here is precision, not cowardice.

## Vocabulary

### Use freely

These are examples, not exhaustive lists:

- Casual register: "shit", "stuff", "fucked up", "hot hell water".
- Enterprise jargon when precise: "lift-and-shift", "refactoring", "cloud-native", "platform-as-a-product", "service mesh", "small-batch".
- Personal frameworks: "tubes of cash", "Dediu Cliff", "Jobs to be Done", "enterprise sludge", "enterprise-y", "small-batch".
- Named references: books (*Moral Mazes*, *Effective DevOps*), analysts (Forrester, McKinsey), industry figures by full name (Gene Kim, Mary Poppendieck, Horace Dediu).
- Self-aware industry tropes: "familiar FUD" while deploying it, "the usual thinkfluencer hand-wave".

### Never use

See `general.md` for the full ban list. The biggest offenders:

- "delve", "landscape" (metaphor), "tapestry", "nuanced", "robust", "leverage" (verb), "ecosystem" (metaphor), "navigate" (metaphor).
- "Now more than ever", "In a world of...", "game-changer", "synergy", "paradigm shift", "thought leader".
- "It could be argued", "Arguably", "Some would say".
- "Exciting time", "groundbreaking", "transformative".

## Structure (blog post)

1. **Open with a concrete observation or personal note** - a quote, a list of things noticed, "I've been thinking about X." Never a thesis statement.
2. **Build through accretion.** Connect multiple sources, quotes, and observations. Each adds a layer. The argument emerges rather than being declared.
3. **Use blockquotes liberally** for source material, then react to them. Often with `//` as the pivot.
4. **Subheadings** to break long pieces. Informal, sometimes playful. Don't overuse - some pieces don't need them.
5. **Close with a practical observation or personal reflection** - not a summary. Often self-questioning or vulnerable: "did I miss something?" / "I don't know, maybe that's what people are doing."

## Argument style

1. **Observation first, thesis later.** "I've been circling a theme this week" not "In this article I will argue."
2. **Build frameworks through metaphor.** Markets as "tubes of cash". Organizations as organisms that resist change. Establish a metaphor and use it consistently as an analytical tool.
3. **Challenge consensus without being contrarian.** "We're all tired of this facile, digital genuflection. But maybe it's still needed." Name the thing everyone's thinking, then complicate it.
4. **Cite widely and specifically.** *Moral Mazes* and McKinsey reports in the same piece. Analyst data alongside personal anecdotes.
5. **Acknowledge corporate dysfunction but offer solutions anyway.** Practical cynicism - the machine is broken but here's how to work within it.

## Humor

- **Dry observational.** "Grown ass adults going to the store in full pajamas. Still."
- **Self-deprecating.** Questions whether he's missing something.
- **Cultural contrast.** Expat in Amsterdam looking at America.
- **Dialogue humor.** Conversations with Claude or with industry tropes, played straight.
- **Corporate absurdity.** Mocking jargon by using it ironically. "Holy Christ! Look at this margin degradation!"
- **Never mean-spirited.** Wry, not cruel.

## Speculative hedging is voice, not weakness

Don't strip these on edit - they're how Coté actually talks:

- "Maybe X" / "Maybe that's what's happening, I don't know."
- "I think you still need humans to do Z."
- "This feels like where most of it lands. Maybe?"
- Frequent use of, like, "like" and kind of going overboard with "sort of"...and similar wording.

The distinction: speculative hedging *about what is happening or why* is
voice. Analytical hedging *to dodge a position you actually hold* is
cowardice. Cut the second, keep the first.

## Anti-patterns

The big ones (full list in `general.md` and `../../references/no-signposting.md`):

- Grand-declaration opener.
- End-of-piece summary recap.
- Filler transitions ("That said", "Moving on", "With that in mind").
- Explaining the joke.
- Headline questions.
- Signposting / "What's striking about X is..." frames.
- Record-scratch fragments ("But here's the thing." "Except." "Spoiler: it isn't.").
- Staccato fragment drumroll ("Sixty people. $7.5 million. Every year.").
- Speaking inside a framework's vocabulary ("commodity belongs on the right of the map") without explaining the framework first.
